Who was born in your town?

166,586 notable people in Wikidata, matched to the 3,869 UK towns and cities they were born in. The most famous person from each, the towns that punch above their weight, and what all these people did.

Dr Tristan Hotham, Independent researcher · 2026 · Data: Wikidata (CC0); Wikimedia page views; ONS, NRS and NISRA populations; OS Open Names

26%of matched births were in London, home to 14% of the people in these towns today
18%are women. In each town's single most famous slot, women hold 14%
Read this first: notable, not typical; and "born in" is not always "from". The people here are those famous enough to have a Wikidata entry with a birthplace: they lean towards men, sport, politics and the professions. Since the mid 20th century most British babies have been born in hospital, so a birthplace is often the hospital town, not the home town. That is why small towns with large hospitals rank so high for people born since 1950. Places come from Wikidata's own birthplace items, which are only as good as whoever entered them. I matched them to modern built-up areas by their coordinates; 19,908 people whose birthplace is just "England", "Scotland", a county or similar could not be placed.

Every town has a famous son or daughter, and most of them have a sign or a plaque to prove it. I wanted to see the whole country at once: who is the most famous person born in each place, and which places produce far more notable people than their size would suggest?

I took every person in Wikidata whose place of birth is in the United Kingdom: 200,835 people, from medieval kings to footballers born this century. I placed 166,586 of them in one of 4,979 towns and cities with an official population, and measured fame by how many language editions of Wikipedia (and sister sites) have a page about them. Search your town on the map, or read on for what stands out.

    02 · The famousShakespeare, Newton, Darwin: and your town's

    Ranked by the number of Wikimedia sites with a page about them, the most famous people born in the UK are William Shakespeare (Stratford-upon-Avon, 336 sites), Isaac Newton (Colsterworth, 303) and Charles Darwin (Shrewsbury, 279). Wikidata records Newton's birthplace as Woolsthorpe Manor, which falls inside the built-up area of the nearby village of Colsterworth.

    Town by town the winners are a mixed crowd. Of the 3,869 towns, the most famous person was a sportsperson in 32% of them, worked in science or scholarship in 16%, in politics, law or public life in 10% and in writing in 10%. In 966 towns the most famous person was born in 1950 or later. The two measures of fame often disagree: for the 3,658 towns where I have both, the person with most language editions is also the most read in English in 71%. Languages favour sportspeople (34% of town winners against 28% by English readers) and scientists (15% against 11%); English readers favour actors (12% against 8%) and people born since 1950 (32% against 26%). London's most famous by languages is Charlie Chaplin; its most read is Harry Kane.

    03 · Per headWhich towns punch above their weight?

    Bigger places produce more notable people, and not just in proportion: across the 957 towns of 10,000 people or more, doubling a town's population today goes with roughly 2.5 times as many notable births. Cities draw in the young, the ambitious and, for the last century, the maternity wards. The ten largest towns and cities hold 22% of the people in these towns but 41% of the notable births.

    Per head, among the 192 towns and cities of 50,000 or more, Edinburgh leads with 76 notable births per 10,000 residents today, then Glasgow (75), Belfast (71), Liverpool (70) and Oxford (67). The median is 25. At the other end are the post-war new towns: Cumbernauld (3.4), Corby (3.4), Bracknell (3.7) and Milton Keynes (3.8). They had few residents to be born there before the 1950s, and their babies were often born in a hospital elsewhere.

    Born before 1900, the list is a roll call of old county and cathedral towns, ports and university seats: Armagh, Carmarthen, Montrose, Caernarfon and Dumbarton among towns of 10,000 to 50,000; Edinburgh and Bath among the larger places. Born since 1950, it becomes a list of hospital towns.

    04 · The hospital effectBorn in Bellshill: when the birthplace is the maternity ward

    Before 1900 most people were born at home, and 9% of the 1800s births in the data that I could place on the map were outside any town I count. Since 1980 that share is 1%: babies are born in hospitals, and hospitals are in towns. Wikidata usually records the town, so the hospital town gets the credit.

    Some small towns show it starkly. Across all towns, 37% of notable births with a known year are since 1950. In Pembury it is 90%, in Ascot 88%, in Frimley 81% and in Cuckfield 77%. Several of the towns at the top of this list are home to, or were home to, large hospitals with maternity units serving a wide area. Bellshill has 292 notable births for 19,700 residents, 74% of them since 1950. The rankings across eras agree only loosely: for towns of 10,000 to 50,000, the rank correlation between births per head before 1900 and since 1950 is 0.59.

    05 · What they didFootballers, scientists and the military towns

    I sorted each person into one broad group from their Wikidata occupations. Sport is the biggest single group: 14% are footballers and 19% other sportspeople. Science, scholarship and medicine (including historians, university teachers and doctors) is 13%, writing 9%, politics, law and public life 8%, acting 7% and music 7%. The mix changes sharply over time: the military and the church fade, sport rises.

    Per head, each group has its capitals among towns of 20,000 or more. For footballers it is Dumbarton (72 born there), Irvine and Bishop Auckland; for other sports Pontefract, where 116 of the 138 sportspeople are listed as a rugby league player, and Neath; for science and scholarship the university cities Edinburgh, Aberdeen, Oxford and Cambridge; for the military, the Clyde: Greenock, Dumbarton and Falkirk.

    06 · WomenFrom one in ten to one in four

    Women are 18% of the notable people with a recorded sex. The share was 10% for people born in the 1700s, 14% in the 1800s, 20% in the 1900s and 28% for those born since 2000. Among the very famous (50 or more language sites) it is 18%. The share by group runs from 2% among footballers to 42% in acting and 35% in writing.

    07 · Four nationsScotland punches hardest

    Per head of today's population, Scotland has 43.6 notable births per 10,000 residents in its towns, Wales 35.6, Northern Ireland 34.2 and England 24.9. England's figure includes London at 49.2. I cannot tell from these data how much of the gap is history and how much is coverage: Wikidata's lists of some groups, footballers among them, are far more complete for some leagues and nations than others; and Scotland's populations come from a different statistical geography (NRS localities) from England's and Wales's (ONS built-up areas). Women's share is lowest in Scotland (13%) and highest in England (18%).

    08 · MethodHow I did this, and what to watch for

    People. Wikidata, queried on 29 October 2026 through the QLever SPARQL endpoint: every human (Q5) with a place of birth (P19) whose country (P17) is the United Kingdom or a predecessor state, 200,835 people. For each I kept one birthplace, the earliest birth year, sex, occupations and the number of Wikimedia sitelinks. 1,072 people have more than one birthplace recorded; one was used.

    Towns. England and Wales: ONS built-up areas (2022) with Census 2021 residents summed from the LSOAs best-fitted to each area; the 34 London borough built-up areas are merged into one London, because most London births are recorded simply as "London". Scotland: NRS localities with mid-2020 estimates, placed at a built-up area or OS Open Names place of the same name. Northern Ireland: NISRA Census 2021 settlements of 500 or more, placed at a Wikidata settlement of the same name. 4,979 towns in all, with 61,590,925 residents.

    Matching. Each birthplace's coordinates go to the town whose equal-area circle (plus a margin) contains them, measured from the area's centre and from any OS Open Names place of the same name nearby; in England and Wales a birthplace left over goes to the built-up area of the nearest neighbourhood (MSOA) centre within 2 km. Birthplaces recorded as the UK, a nation, a county or a council area are too coarse (19,908 people), 10,089 were in the countryside or in places too small to count, and 4,014 were outside the UK as it is now (mostly in the Republic of Ireland before 1922). 742 birthplace names in the data, such as Newport, Richmond and Bangor, are shared by two or more places; Wikidata links people to a place item, not a name, but a wrong link made by an editor would put someone in the wrong town, and 33% of matched births are at such a place.

    Fame. Sitelinks count the Wikimedia sites with a page about the person. They favour people known around the world, and groups such as sportspeople whose pages are created in many language editions. As a check I gathered average daily views of the English Wikipedia article over 30 days for the three people with most sitelinks at each birthplace (21,191 articles). Occupations are grouped by which group holds most of a person's Wikidata occupations.

    Limits. Notability in Wikidata follows Wikipedia and other databases: sportspeople and politicians are very well covered, women and working people of the past much less so. Births across all centuries are set against today's population. Hospital births credit hospital towns. Only the most famous and most read person per town is named on this page.
